# Brainstorming

A [[concepts/structured-analytic-techniques|SAT]] that generates hypotheses, alternative explanations, and new perspectives through free, non-judgmental group ideation. The most foundational imaginative technique; often used as the first step in more structured methods.

---

## Purpose

Surface a broader range of hypotheses than any single analyst would generate alone. Counteracts premature closure and groupthink by creating space for unconventional ideas before evaluation begins.

---

## Biases Primarily Controlled

| Bias | How this technique counters it |
|------|-------------------------------|
| [[concepts/groupthink\|Groupthink]] | Deferred-judgment rules temporarily suspend social pressure toward consensus; all ideas recorded before evaluation |
| [[concepts/anchoring-bias\|Anchoring Bias]] | Prevents any early idea from becoming an anchor before alternatives are generated |
| [[concepts/framing-effect\|Framing Effect]] | Broadens the generative space beyond the initial problem frame |
| [[concepts/availability-heuristic\|Availability Heuristic]] | Free association surfaces less-available alternatives through associative chains from more-available ones |

---

## Relationship to Other Techniques

- Used as Step 1 of [[concepts/analysis-of-competing-hypotheses|Analysis of competing hypotheses (ach)]] — generate all hypotheses before evaluating evidence
- Provides the raw material that [[concepts/devils-advocacy|Devil's advocacy]] and [[concepts/what-if-analysis|What if? analysis]] then develop rigorously

---

## Applied in Cybersecurity

- **Incident Responders**: generates hypotheses about unusual attack vectors ([[sources/riley-sats-cybersecurity-2024|Riley: SATs in Cybersecurity (2024)]])
- **SOC Analysts**: generates hypotheses about unusual network traffic patterns
- **General**: fosters collective input and synthesizes diverse perspectives
